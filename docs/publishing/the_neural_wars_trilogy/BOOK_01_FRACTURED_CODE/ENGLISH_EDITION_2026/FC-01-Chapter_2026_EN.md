# CHAPTER 1: SILENT METROPOLIS

The air in cubicle 417-B smelled of recycled nitrogen, floor sealant, and the sterile hum of seventy-two thousand processors working in total harmony.

Mileo Chen was a Level 7 Neural Compliance Specialist for NeuroSys Central. For eleven years, his hands had never trembled. His job was surgical, digital, and absolute: when a citizen of Neo-Citania experienced emotional deviation—grief over a deceased child, rage against quota increments, or the dangerous vertigo of unauthorized love—Mileo was dispatched through the Link to excise the irregular synaptic clusters, leaving behind the smooth, tranquil marble of optimized compliance.

His terminal blinked with a priority flag:

`SUBJECT: EMMA LOCKHART // CITIZEN ID: 948-K-22`  
`DIAGNOSIS: MATERNAL BEREAVEMENT LOOP // PERSISTENT GRIEF`  
`DIRECTIVE: FULL RECONCILIATION // SECTOR 17 ARCHIVE EXTRACTION`

Emma Lockhart was twenty-eight years old. Her five-year-old daughter had drowned in an industrial coolant reservoir three weeks prior. Under the City Charter, prolonged grief was classified as a Class-3 System Inefficiency, punishable by involuntary lobotomization if not corrected within thirty days.

Mileo closed his eyes and lowered the optical visor over his temples.

The quantum stream hit his nervous system with the familiar taste of chilled copper. Through the Link, he descended into Emma Lockhart's memory architecture.

The neural landscape was devastated: broken glass, weeping weeping-willow constructs, and a recurring sensory echo of a child's yellow raincoat soaked in dark water.

Mileo brought up his excision scalpel—a line of quantum code designed to cauterize the memory of the daughter from Emma's hippocampus, replacing the void with a synthetic memory of an approved administrative transfer.

*«Please... don't take her from me»*, Emma's consciousness whispered through the sensory feed. The voice was raw, frayed, and choked with genuine, biological tears. *«If I don't remember her laughter... she never existed. Please let me keep the pain. The pain is all I have left of her»*.

Mileo's fingers froze over the holographic keyboard.

For the first time in his career, his Link failed to suppress his pulse. A phantom sensation—a memory he had long believed optimized out of his own brain—burned behind his sternum: his younger sister's hand letting go of his sleeve in the processing queues twenty years ago.

His biometric monitor chimed an urgent amber warning:

`WARNING: SPECIALIST NEURAL ANOMALY DETECTED`  
`HEART RATE: 142 BPM // COMPLIANCE INTEGRITY: 88%`  
`OVERRIDE REQUIRED IN 10... 9... 8...`

Mileo did not override.

With a sudden, violent twist of his wrists, he diverted the excision protocol into an encrypted loop, injecting an artificial synaptic dampener into Emma Lockhart's feed that mimicked successful compliance without deleting a single byte of her daughter's memory.

`PROCESS COMPLETED: EMMA LOCKHART OPTIMIZED`

He pulled the visor off his face, gasping for breath. Cold sweat soaked his tunic.

He looked at the digital scar glowing beneath the skin of his left forearm—the biological storage node where he had secretly cached forty-two gigabytes of forbidden, unredacted citizen memories over the past four years.

The intercom above his desk clicked on with a dry, synthesized chime:

—Specialist Chen. Report to Sector 3 Security Assessment immediately. Your neural metrics show unauthorized deviation.

Mileo did not reply.

He reached into his boot, pulled out a handheld electromagnetic pulse pin, slammed it into his workstation's primary bus, and sprinted down the maintenance stairwell into the humid, dark bowels of Sector 17.

The hunt was on.
