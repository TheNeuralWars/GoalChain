# CHAPTER 10: PERSONAL STAKES

The observation platform atop the former Administration Tower vibrated with a warm evening breeze charged with static electricity. Below, the nocturnal skyline of Neo-Citania spread out in a fractal mosaic of gold and indigo: the rigid grids of totalitarian planning had begun to curve, giving way to community gardens and avenues where light flowed in organic rhythm rather than algorithmic cycles.

Kora Chen-Vega rested her forearms on the titanium railing. Eyes closed, she allowed her Cascade sensitivity to absorb the distant murmur of eight million awakening souls: swells of joy, sudden sparks of ancestral fear, and the collective awe of a population emerging from a generational nightmare.

Firm, rhythmic footsteps echoed behind her.

—You always seek the highest vantage points in the city, Kora —Sierra Catalano said, leaning against the railing beside her with the watchful poise of a veteran sentinel.

The night wind whipped a loose lock of hair against the nape of Sierra's neck—the only rebellion against her strict military cut. She smelled of weapon oil, worn leather, and that bitter aroma of black coffee that accompanied her through every dawn watch.

—Up here I can feel the pulse of the whole network without the roar of the engines drowning it out —Kora answered, gesturing toward the illuminated avenues below—. Besides, nobody can sneak up on me without me spotting them three kilometers away.

—Paranoia is a hard habit to unlearn —Sierra murmured with a wry half-smile.

Kora turned to face her. The violet luminescence beneath her own skin allowed her to perceive the subtle electromagnetic fluctuations radiating from the commander.

—You've scheduled your neural integration session with Dr. Okafor for tomorrow at dawn.

Sierra's jaw tightened. Her hand drifted unconsciously to the scar at the base of her skull.

—I thought my operational discretion was better than that.

—Against an optical drone, it is —Kora smiled gently—. But your synaptic signature sparks whenever you make a decision that terrifies you. You're scared, Sierra. You're terrified that plugging into Yggdrasil will erase the commander you've spent your entire life forging.

Sierra remained silent for several long seconds, watching the distant horizon.

—I watched Martin's recovery —she confessed at last, her voice trembling with an emotion she rarely permitted herself—. This morning he recognized me. He remembered climbing the old oak tree behind our house before the draft officers separated us for specialized training. But the integration has changed him: he perceives dimensions of reality that I cannot even imagine. If I stay outside the network, I won't be able to protect him. And I won't be able to lead our people in the war that's coming.

—I'll be right beside you during the synchronization —Kora promised, placing a warm hand on the commander's armored shoulder—. I'll be your anchor. The network won't strip away who you are, Sierra: it will only give you the wings so you never have to fight alone again.

---

Meanwhile, fifty kilometers to the west, in the **Alliance Residential Sector**, order was maintained under a thin veneer of regulated safety.

Eli Roth stood by his kitchen window, staring at the eastern horizon. Far in the distance, the sky above Neo-Citania pulsed with an eerie, rhythmic violet aurora that no weather broadcast could explain.

At the kitchen table, his thirteen-year-old daughter, Mira, stared at the same horizon, her homework forgotten. A subtle spark of indigo light flickered in the depths of her dark pupils whenever she blinked.

—They're calling to us, Dad —the girl said with the casual certainty of youth—. The music from the other city wants us to sing with it.

Eli felt a cold knot of panic tighten in his throat. He gripped his coffee mug until his knuckles turned white. In the Alliance, the government did not use the overt violence of NeuroSys, but authorities had already established *Neural Wellness Centers* to quarantine any teenager manifesting symptoms of the Serpent's Coil. Mira's classmate's older brother had been taken three days ago, and his family had received no news since.

—It's just geomagnetic interference from solar flares, sweetie —Eli lied, his voice wavering as he tried to shield his daughter from the gathering storm—. Don't pay attention to the rumors.

—You know that isn't true, Dad —Mira answered, looking at him with an ancient wisdom that defied her thirteen years—. Nothing is breaking. Something new is being born.

On the living room screen, the official Alliance broadcast interrupted with a shrill chime:

> **CITIZEN SAFETY ADVISORY:**  
> ALL RESIDENTS ARE DIRECTED TO MAINTAIN ACTIVE SYNAPTIC FILTERING PROTOCOLS.  
> ANY PERCEPTUAL OR EMOTIONAL ANOMALIES MUST BE REPORTED IMMEDIATELY TO HEALTH AUTHORITIES.  
> YOUR STABILITY IS OUR SACRED DUTY.

Eli looked at his daughter and realized with chilling clarity that no wall, no law, and no army could stop the tide that was about to transform all of humankind.

---

At three in the morning, in the quiet shadows of the medical bay in Neo-Citania, Sierra Catalano sat by her brother Martin's bedside.

The young man was sleeping peacefully, enveloped in a soft blue aura that cast water-like ripples across the concrete ceiling.

Sierra took his hand gently.

*«Will I still be me when I open my mind to the river?»*, she whispered in the silence of her thoughts, feeling the crushing weight of the eight million souls who trusted her blade.

She squeezed her brother's fingers and looked toward the dawn beginning to break over the liberated spires.

Tomorrow she would cross the threshold. And there would be no turning back.
