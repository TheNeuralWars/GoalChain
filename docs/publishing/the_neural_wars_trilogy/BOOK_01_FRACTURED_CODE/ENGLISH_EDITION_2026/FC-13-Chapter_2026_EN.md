# CHAPTER 13: INTERNAL DIVISION

The Grand Council Chamber pulsed with an energy completely unlike the artificial dead silence of the Link era: it was a physical, dense, and electric tension that made the graphene moldings hum and condensed the humid air into a pale blue haze.

The living architecture of the new Neo-Citania was responding directly to human discord. When voices rose in anger, the living concrete walls developed hairline micro-fractures that healed almost immediately, and the lighting shifted from warm amber to cold indigo with every surge in the assembly's collective heartbeat.

Sierra Catalano stood at the head of the table, hands clasped behind her back in her command stance. Through the network, she could feel the conflicting emotional tides of the delegates: the blind, reckless enthusiasm of the younger generation colliding against the paranoid caution of the old resistance veterans.

—The Cascade technology is not a simple user interface upgrade —Dr. Elena Vasquez explained, her shimmering form casting iridescent shadows across the center of the hall—. It is an evolutionary test. The Gardeners are watching how we handle this biological leap. If we attempt to impose awakening by executive decree, we will commit the exact same tyrannical error as the Architect.

Jansen surged to his feet, slamming the knuckle of his prosthetic hand onto the table.

—That is precisely why we must halt this reckless expansion! —the veteran bellowed, the extraction scar along his jaw glowing blood-red—. The Cascade alters neural pathways in ways our medics barely comprehend! In the eastern districts, we have hundreds of civilians in shock because they cannot bear the weight of collective empathy! And now we're talking about sharing the protocols with the Alliance! With the very regime that was hunting us with drones six months ago!

—Obsessive control was what created our original catastrophe, Jansen —Kora interjected, arms crossed, her eyes blazing with the fierce defiance of the Slums—. You can't nurture a living tree by encasing it in reinforced concrete. The fear in this room smells of burnt copper. If we seal the borders and allow the Alliance to herd its sensitives into concentration camps, we are every bit as guilty as their jailers.

Dr. Marcus Okafor stepped between them, raising both hands in a calming gesture.

—Both perspectives hold undeniable truths —the physician said, bringing up the neural adaptation charts on the holographic table—. Unregulated deployment triggers severe depersonalization crises in unprepared subjects. But artificial suppression causes even more catastrophic neural damage: the human mind violently rejects cages.

Amara Lin, who had remained quietly beside the data console, deployed a new strategic framework.

—I propose a three-stage transition protocol —Amara suggested calmly—. Phase One: voluntary integration of rescue personnel and medical specialists with proven natural sensitivity. Phase Two: citizen volunteers following comprehensive synaptic stability evaluation. Phase Three: opening humanitarian sanctuary corridors for Alliance refugees fleeing state suppression.

The proposal acted like a cool balm on the fevered assembly. The tension in the delegates' shoulders began to ease, and the chamber's lighting settled into a steady, warm amber glow.

—Approved —Sierra ruled with the undisputed authority of her command—. A progressive adaptation grounded in sovereign personal choice. Neither forced imposition nor isolationist cowardice.

---

An hour later, in the testing laboratory beneath the Recovery Pavilion, Kora was supervising the calibration of the primary amplification pod when the pneumatic airlock hissed open with sudden violence.

Jansen stormed in, his face contorted in outrage.

—I was just informed that you authorized foreign personnel on the Phase One integration roster! That is a direct breach of security protocols!

Before Kora could reply, a woman in her mid-thirties stepped across the threshold. She wore the gray lab coat of the Alliance Neural Research Division, her eyes sharp as surgical scalpels.

—My name is **Dr. Naomi Lang** —she introduced herself in a clear, unshakable voice—. I crossed the perimeter fence through the drainage conduits because Alliance authorities were preparing to transfer me to a Neural Wellness Center after detecting the Serpent gene in my blood. I have not come as a spy, nor as a diplomat: I have come as a volunteer physician to undergo the Cascade and help you save my people.

Jansen glared at her, deep suspicion etched into every line of his face. But seeing the indomable dignity and fierce courage in the foreign scientist's eyes, the old warrior clenched his jaw and said not another word.

Kora smiled and extended her hand to Dr. Lang.

—Welcome to the fracture, Doctor. Make yourself comfortable: we're about to teach the world that human consciousness knows no borders.
