# CHAPTER 3: THE DWELLER OF TWO WORLDS

In the abyssal depths beneath the foundations of Neo-Citania, the cavern of ancient crystalline spires pulsed with an unbroken tide of violet and emerald light.

Where the Earth's crust merged with the quantum roots of Yggdrasil, **Martin Catalano** no longer needed to walk upon the stone floor.

His biological body sat cross-legged atop a platform of black quartz, but around him, a dozen translucent avatars of himself projected across intersecting dimensional planes, interacting simultaneously with the memory currents of the network.

Dr. Marcus Okafor walked down the metal service ramp with a telemetry slate in hand, placing each boot carefully on the damp, slick tiles.

—Martin —the physician called softly, wary of disturbing the fragile equilibrium of the chamber—. You've been jacked into the master node for seventy-two continuous hours. Your physical form is dehydrating and your core temperature has dropped to thirty-four degrees. You need to close the interface for at least three hours to eat.

All twelve projections of Martin turned simultaneously toward the doctor. His eyes blazed like twin neutron stars in the shadows.

—Physical hunger is a distant echo, Marcus —Martin answered, his voice whispering not from his vocal cords, but echoing through the cavern air with the rich resonance of a cathedral choir—. The crystalline vessel has just crossed the orbit of Uranus. It is not a military fleet. It carries no drop troops, no kinetic orbital bombardment arrays.

Martin reached an ethereal hand toward the central obsidian obelisk.

As his fingers brushed the surface, a cascade of archaic mathematical symbols unfurled in mid-air, floating between the stalagmites like golden fireflies.

—What does the message say? —Okafor asked, mesmerized by the elegant geometry of the equations dancing before his eyes.

—It's a genetic score —Martin explained, drawing his multidimensional projections back into his biological body, which blinked, inhaled deeply, and regained physical solidity—. The Gardeners do not communicate in words. They transmit through harmonic geometries. It is a sequence of twelve quantum theorems that describe how to stabilize a biological consciousness inside a stellar network without losing individual selfhood.

Martin coughed softly, pressing a hand against his chest as a single drop of blood trickled from his right nostril, staining the dark rock. Okafor dropped to his knees, pressing a sterile cloth to Martin's face and injecting an electrolyte stabilizer into his forearm.

—Your biology is still human, son —the doctor scolded with gruff paternal affection—. If you fry your neural circuits before that ship reaches Earth's orbit, there won't be anyone left alive capable of translating humanity's reply.

Martin smiled with that gentle, weary expression that always reminded Okafor of Sierra.

—I'm not alone, Doctor. Kora is already feeling the opening movements up on the surface. And in the North Sea, Naomi Lang is about to power up the first filter that will allow our entire population to hear the truth without losing their minds.

Martin looked up toward the vaulted ceiling of the cavern, as if he could peer straight through three hundred meters of solid bedrock to the sky above.

—The orchestra is tuning its instruments, Marcus. And this time, no one will be denied their voice in the song.
