# CHAPTER 16: THE YES OF THE FRACTURED

The global referendum lasted twenty-four terrestrial hours.

There were no plastic ballot boxes guarded by armed troops, no opaque counting algorithms.

Through the sovereign quantum voting protocol engineered by Naomi Lang and the engineers of Neo-Citania, every citizen of Earth cast their choice through a conscious pulse of pure intention: *Yes, we accept the invitation* or *No, we choose to remain in isolation*.

In the Global Coordination Hub in Neo-Citania, Sierra Catalano and Kora Chen-Vega stood before the planetary holographic map.

Slowly, every corner of the Earth began to kindle with points of golden light: from the working-class districts of South America to the megacities of Asia, from the Pacific archipelagos to the valleys of Europe and Africa.

It was not a unanimous vote: twelve percent of the global population chose to keep their communities outside the interstellar network, and their rights, autonomy, and borders were fiercely protected and honored by the Free Assembly.

Yet the remaining eighty-eight percent of humanity—seven billion souls—voted with their hearts laid bare.

The final tally consolidated across the main display:

`PLANETARY SOVEREIGN REFERENDUM: APPROVED // EARTH ACCEPTS THE INVITATION`

In the streets of Neo-Citania and across every city on Earth, a celebration erupted unlike anything previous generations had ever known: acoustic and electronic music on every street corner, embraces between former soldiers and underground rebels, and tears of profound relief shed beneath a sky that no longer inspired fear.

Kora turned to Sierra, tears streaming down the commander's weathered cheeks.

—We did it, Commander —Kora whispered, her throat tight with emotion—. We aren't fugitives anymore. We are the ambassadors of a new world.

Sierra pulled Kora into a fierce embrace, feeling the heartbeat of an entire planet taking its first steps as a cosmic civilization.

—No, Kora —Sierra smiled through her tears—. We aren't just ambassadors. We are living proof that the fracture was the only way for the light to get in.
