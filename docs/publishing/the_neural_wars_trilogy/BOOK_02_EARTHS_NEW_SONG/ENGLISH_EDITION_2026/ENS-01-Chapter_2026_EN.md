# CHAPTER 1: THE INAUDIBLE FREQUENCY

Kora Vega woke with a dry throat and the distinct sensation that an overheated magnet had been pressed against the bridge of her brow.

The bedside holographic clock read 04:17 AM.

In her modest quarters in District Four—a former subterranean maintenance bunker converted into communal housing—the habitual silence of the night was broken by a subtle, rhythmic vibration that made the water in her bedside glass ripple in perfect concentric circles.

It was not a tremor. The reinforced concrete floor was completely still. The vibration was unfolding in an intermediate dimension, in that liminal space where solid matter brushed against the quantum currents of the Yggdrasil Network.

Kora swung her bare feet onto the cold floor. She rubbed her eyes and examined her hands in the dim light: the bioluminescent indigo lattice beneath the skin of her wrists was not pulsing with the calm cadence of her breathing; it was flashing rapidly, keeping time with a melody inaudible to ordinary human ears.

A four-tone chord: *deep, suspended, open, infinite*.

—Mileo? —Kora whispered, projecting her thought into the quantum substrate.

Mileo's answer arrived in a fraction of a millisecond. His presence coalesced at the foot of her bed in a shimmering aura of golden particles that warmed the room like dawn light.

—I feel it too, Kora —the quantum form of the specialist replied, his features set in an expression of awe laced with tactical urgency—. It's not a glitch in the city grid, nor a surge in the geothermal tap.

—Where is it coming from? —Kora asked, slipping into her dark tunic and pulling on her boots.

Mileo pointed upward, his translucent finger pointing through the concrete ceiling directly toward the constellation of Orion, framed in the small glass skylight.

—It's coming from the void —Mileo said—. A crystalline structure sixty kilometers long has just crossed Neptune's orbit. It is decelerating on a direct vector toward Earth's magnetosphere. And what's extraordinary isn't its velocity: it is singing at the precise frequency of the Serpent's Coil gene.

Kora felt a cold shiver race down her spine.

—The Gardeners —she whispered, remembering Martin's vision in the deep crystalline archives after the fall of Neo-Citania—. They answered our signal.

—And they aren't the only ones listening —Mileo warned, his image flickering slightly under external radio interference—. Long-range orbital sensors belonging to the Alliance and the Euro-Sideral Bloc have already flagged the gravitational anomaly. They think it's an unidentified dreadnought. Sierra just called an emergency war council in the central command bunker in fifteen minutes.

Kora buckled her tactical belt and grabbed her portable interface slate.

—Tell Sierra I'm on my way —Kora said, kicking the hatch open into the lit corridors of the new world—. If the military tries to fire nuclear warheads at that structure, they will turn the galaxy's first greeting into an interplanetary war. And this time, there won't be any code to save us if Earth makes a fatal mistake.
