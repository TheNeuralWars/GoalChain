# CHAPTER 2: SILICON BORDERS

The Palace of Nations in Geneva smelled of aged floor wax, acid rain evaporating off armored glass, and the sharp metallic tang radiating from the synaptic inhibition suits worn by the Euro-Siberian Alliance diplomats.

Sierra Catalano walked down the grand marble gallery flanked by four escorts from The Fractured. She wore the formal black coat and graphite pauldrons of the Free Human Alliance—no medals or ribbons, save for the thin, jagged extraction scar tracing the left side of her throat.

Through the reinforced bay windows of the grand hall, Lake Geneva lay cleaved in two by a colossal floating barrier two hundred meters tall: the **Faraday Wall of Geneva**.

A sprawling lattice of copper and tantalum humming at a constant sixty hertz, engineered to block every quantum particle drifting from the liberated sectors of Neo-Citania and keep the European population locked in synthetic, sedated compliance.

Sierra pushed the heavy oak doors open and stepped into the plenary hall.

The murmur of seventy delegates died instantly. In the front rows, ministers of the Technology Consortium adjusted their high collars, glaring at her with that mixture of terror and disdain tyrants always reserve for those who have proven that chains can be broken.

Chancellor Viktor Vance, an elderly technocrat whose skull was reinforced with a lattice of spun gold connecting his memories to a private corporate cloud, addressed the assembly from the central podium.

—The presence of the Neo-Citania delegation at this summit does not constitute recognition of your sovereignty, Commander Catalano —Vance droned through harmonic voice synthesizers—. You are here solely to receive the terms of the Neural Containment Treaty. The deep-space signal your repeaters are broadcasting into Earth's atmosphere is an existential threat to global security.

Sierra did not take the seat assigned to her. She stood in the center of the floor, hands clasped behind her back, locking eyes with the chancellor.

—We aren't broadcasting anything, Vance —Sierra replied in a clear voice that carried effortlessly without a microphone—. The frequency originates from the Kuiper Belt, and it is slicing through your copper walls as if they were wet tissue paper. Your own physicists know it is not a weapon. It is a greeting. And every day you spend locking your sensitives in chemical isolation wards, you are committing crimes against the evolution of our species.

—It is an extraterrestrial invasion! —bellowed the Asian Federation delegate, slamming his interface rings against the podium—. We demand the immediate deployment of the heavy orbital defense network to vaporize the object before it crosses the orbit of Mars!

—If you fire heavy kinetic warheads at that structure, you will trigger a quantum feedback pulse that will fry the nervous systems of three hundred million citizens still tethered to legacy Links in your territories —Sierra warned, stepping toward the dais—. We demand the immediate dismantling of the Faraday barriers and the universal right to free consciousness.

In that microsecond, before any conventional sensor could sound an alarm, Sierra's vision flared violet.

Her Cascade perception, sharpened through months of communion with Yggdrasil, revealed the immediate future in a split-second timeline collapse: two stealth infiltration drones hovering in the shadowed rafters above, arming beryllium fragmentation flechettes targeted directly at her chest.

Sierra did not shout. She did not hesitate.

Pivoting on her heel with superhuman speed, she drew her arc pistol and fired two plasma bolts straight into the dark ceiling moldings.

Twin explosions lit the gilded dome. The two drones crashed in blazing wreckage across the Euro-Siberian table, spewing thick black smoke and incandescent shrapnel.

Panic ripped through the hall. Diplomats threw themselves to the floor as security details scrambled with weapons raised.

Sierra reholstered her weapon with cold precision, looked down at Chancellor Vance cowering behind his bulletproof glass podium, and said with razor-sharp calm:

—Next time you want to negotiate, Chancellor, make sure your assassins don't broadcast on the same frequency as your lies. The Earth is going to hear the song, with or without your permission.

She turned on her heel and strode out of the palace beneath the wailing sirens, her eyes fixed on the sky that was beginning to sing.
