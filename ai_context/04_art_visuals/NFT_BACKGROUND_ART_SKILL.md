## 2. THE LOW-ANGLE STADIUM & BOKEH PROTOCOLS (PREVENTING CLUTTER)
To prevent the stadium details from clashing with the player card assets, you **MUST** strictly adhere to the following composition rules:

1. **GROUND-LEVEL CAMERA & TEXTURED TURF (MANDATORY)**:
   - Always position the camera at a **very low, ground-level camera perspective**. 
   - The lower third of the frame must be dominated by a `"highly detailed, textured dark soccer pitch turf (grass)"`, providing a solid ground for the player.
2. **HEAVY CINEMATIC BOKEH (MANDATORY)**:
   - Always describe the background stadium as having `"extremely heavy depth of field, with the distant stadium grandstands and roof structures strongly out of focus, dissolving into a soft, buttery, professional bokeh blur"`.
   - Use photographic anchors like `"shot with a premium 35mm lens at a wide-open aperture of f/1.4 to keep the foreground turf grounded while dissolving distant details into a soft texture"`.
3. **SPARSE STRUCTURAL LED LIGHTING (SOBER LIGHTS)**:
   - Never use classic stadium floodlights or blinding white lights.
   - Describe lighting as `"thin, low-intensity recessed LED strips outlining the stadium's sweeping architectural contours"` or `"soft, dim, barely-visible volumetric atmospheric mist rising gently from the pitch in the distance"`.
   - Keep each background strictly monochromatic to its respective rarity color, emerging from dark charcoal shadows.
4. **MANDATORY VERTICAL 3:2 ASPECT RATIO (MANDATORY)**:
   - Describe the camera composition as `"strictly vertical 3:2 portrait aspect ratio"` or `"vertical portrait framing"`.
   - Append the parameter `--ar 2:3` or the phrase `"aspect ratio 2:3"` to enforce the correct vertical orientation.
