# 🎭 Character Psychology, Voice & Dialogue as Weaponry

> "Dialogue is not speech. Dialogue is action between characters. Every line is a tactical strike to change another human being's mind or defend a wound." — David Mamet
> "If you remove the speech tags and you can't tell who is speaking, your characters are mannequins with megaphones."

This intelligence module governs characterization, vocal fingerprints, and psychological subtext.

---

## 🧠 I. The Character Trinity: Wound, Lie, Need

Memorable characters are not defined by quirky hair, tattoos, or combat skills. They are defined by internal psychological architecture:

```
[ THE ORIGINAL WOUND (GHOST) ]
            │
            ▼
[ THE PROTECTIVE LIE ] ◄──── "If I never trust anyone, I can never be broken."
            │
            ├────────► [ THE CONSCIOUS WANT ] (External Goal: "Steal the encryption key")
            │
            └────────► [ THE UNCONSCIOUS NEED ] (Internal Growth: "Sacrifice pride for another")
```

1. **The Wound (The Ghost)**: The traumatic failure, betrayal, or origin breach in their past that haunts their autonomic responses.
2. **The Lie**: The faulty survival heuristic they adopted to cope with the Wound. In high-stakes scenes, their decisions default to defending this Lie.
3. **Want vs. Need**:
   * *Want*: Concrete, visible, external objective.
   * *Need*: Spiritual, emotional, or philosophical shift required to survive the climax.

---

## ⚔️ II. Dialogue as Psychological Warfare

In world-class fiction, **characters never speak to communicate information to the reader**. They speak to:
* Intimidate
* Conceal panic
* Seduce or probe
* Buy critical seconds
* Deflect an uncomfortable truth
* Assert spatial or hierarchical dominance

### The 3 Layers of Every Line:
1. **The Text**: The literal words spilling into the room.
2. **The Subtext**: The primal truth pulsating under the words (fear, lust, hatred, exhaustion).
3. **The Blocking (Physical Action)**: What the hands, eyes, and posture are doing while speaking.

#### Contrast Example:
* ❌ *Amateur / Informational*:
  > "Jansen, you know the Yggdrasil Network is too powerful for us to fight alone. We need their servers or the virus will delete our database by tomorrow morning."
* ✅ *Masterclass / Armed Subtext*:
  > "This is suicide," Jansen says. His fist comes down; the hologram stutters. His northern vowels thicken.
  > "And we're expected to accept that the Yggdrasil Network intends to assist us?"
  > The palm comes down again. The hologram wavers and steadies.

## 🗣️ III. Idiolect: The Vocal Fingerprint

Every major character must possess an unmistakable syntax, cadence, and lexicon:

| Character Archetype | Sentence Velocity | Lexicon & Imagery | Verbal Crutches / Habits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tactical / Veteran** (e.g. Jansen) | Short, heavy monosyllables, blunt verbs, staccato declarations. | Industrial, mechanical, cold steel, physical friction. | Never uses qualifiers (*maybe, probably*). Commands and silences. |
| **Architect / System Engineer** | Longer balanced clauses, clinical terminology, passive voice masking control. | Mathematical, biological decay, systemic latency, vectors. | Answers questions with precise constraints. Speaks around emotion. |
| **Street-Smart / Defiant** | Kinetic bursts, rhythmic interruptions, biting cadence. | High-low slang, somatic metaphors (teeth, bone, neon). | Sarcastic deflection, questions answering questions. |

---

## 🚫 IV. The Anti-Talking-Head Rule (Dynamic Blocking)

Never let two characters talk while floating in a vacuum:
1. **Tool Engagement**: Characters must be checking scopes, unthreading copper cables, resetting circuit breakers, or binding wounds.
2. **Eye Contact as Currency**: A direct stare is an attack; looking away is a strategic retreat or an attempt to hide fear. Track eye vectors with forensic precision.
3. **The Prop Rule**: If an object enters a scene (a data slate, a lighter, an injection cartridge), it must have continuous spatial permanence. Never let a prop disappear or be reused without interaction.
