# ⏱️ Dramatic Causality, Pacing & Scene Triage

> "A story is not a sequence of events linked by 'and then.' It is an inexorable chain forged by 'therefore' and 'but.'" — Robert McKee & Trey Parker
> "Enter late. Leave early." — David Mamet

This intelligence module provides the macro- and micro-engineering needed to sustain hypnotic narrative momentum across a 100,000+ word novel.

---

## ⛓️ I. The Law of Relentless Causality (Therefore / But)

Amateur plots sag because scenes are connected episodically:
* *Amateur Structure*: X happens, **and then** Y happens, **and then** Z happens.
* *Masterclass Structure*: X happens, **THEREFORE** Y must follow, **BUT** an unexpected friction strikes, **THEREFORE** the protagonist is forced into Z.

```
[ ACTION A ] ──( Therefore )──► [ DESIRED OUTCOME ]
                                         │
                                     ( BUT... )
                                         ▼
[ COMPLICATION B ] ◄──( Therefore )─── [ GAP BETWEEN EXPECTATION & REALITY ]
```

### The Invariant Test for Every Chapter
Before editing or validating any chapter, ask:
1. Did the preceding chapter make this scene **inevitable**?
2. Does the climax of this scene force an irreversible choice that ignites the next chapter?
3. If any scene can be reordered without breaking the cause-and-effect chain, **the scene is dead tissue and must be rewritten or excised**.

## 🔄 II. The Scene-and-Sequel Heartbeat (Swain & Butcher)

Fiction breathes in two alternating rhythmic chambers: the **Kinetic Scene** and the **Reflective Sequel**.

### 1. The Kinetic Scene (External Drive — High BPM)
* **Proactive Goal**: The POV character enters with an immediate, quantifiable objective (*"I need to extract the biometric hash before the firewall cycles in 90 seconds"*).
* **Escalating Conflict**: Resistance from opposing willpower, physical environment, or machine error.
* **The Hook/Disaster**: The attempt ends in one of three ways:
  - *No* (Catastrophic failure).
  - *Yes, BUT...* (Success at a crippling unexpected cost).
  - *No, and furthermore...* (Failure compounded by worse stakes).

### 2. The Reflective Sequel (Internal Processing — Controlled Decompression)
* **Visceral Reaction**: Physical collapse, shock, adrenaline dumping, biological consequence.
* **The Dilemma**: No good answers. Choosing between two agonizing sacrifices (not between good and evil).
* **The Decision**: A proactive pivot that formulates the Goal for the next Kinetic Scene.

## ⚡ III. Micro-Tension on Every Page (Donald Maass)

The cardinal sin of mid-tier fiction is the "placid page"—a moment where two allies agree, share facts politely, or wait comfortably for an event.

### The 3 Levers of Constant Friction:
1. **Status Clashing**: Characters in a room are always subtly contesting dominance, authority, or territorial space. Even between lovers or loyal comrades, tension thrums in who holds the door, who interrupts, and who refuses to look.
2. **The Clock / Resource Bleed**: Air is cycling down, heat is venting, batteries are at 14%, a patrol is due in 8 minutes. Physical urgency must press against dialogue.
3. **The Unsaid Secret**: Every character must harbor one fact they are actively trying to keep the other person in the room from discovering.

---

## ✂️ IV. Masterclass Scene Triage (Enter Late, Leave Early)

* **Cut the Front 20% (Throat-Clearing)**: Never start with a character waking up, checking their gear, traveling to a location, or reflecting on the weather. Start mid-sentence, mid-alarm, or mid-breach.
* **Cut the Back 15% (Polite Exits)**: When the emotional turning point or narrative disaster hits, **end the chapter instantly**. Do not describe characters saying goodbye, packing up tools, or walking down corridors. Cut on the apex of the shock.
